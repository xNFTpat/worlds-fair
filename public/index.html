Pool types

DLMM: liquidity sits in price bins. You choose the range and a Spot, Curve or Bid-Ask distribution. Fees depend on swaps reaching your bins.

DAMM v2: a different Meteora pool design. It can use a pool-wide price range or full-range/compounding liquidity. Launch fee schedules and liquidity locks matter; DLMM shape presets do not apply.

Raydium CPMM: full-range liquidity, with LP tokens representing your share. No personal range or DLMM shape presets. DBC / bonding curve: a launch phase which can later migrate to an AMM pool; creating another pool does not prove a token has just launched.

A LITTLE CONTEXT

Clarity before capital.

Providing liquidity (LP) means making tokens available for other people to trade. You can earn a share of trading fees, but token prices, costs and your chosen range all affect the result. Still lets you check the evidence and practise first.

01 / LOOK

Start with a pool

Search for a token, then open its pre-flight. Each check says Pass, Caution or Fail. Open Why? for the evidence. A missing reading stays a caution.

02 / UNDERSTAND

Try a range

Use a suggested range when the pool supports it, or choose a floor below today’s price. See what your SOL could be worth at that floor. The floor does not stop a loss.

03 / PRACTISE

Build a paper portfolio

Add practice SOL and try a basket, staking option or vault. Shared paper strategies can also supply eligible profits to roll into your portfolio.

What do Pass, Caution and Fail mean?
Pass
The available evidence meets this particular screening rule. It is not a promise of profit or a safety certification.
Caution
There is something to review: a missing reading, an older quote, incomplete holder coverage or a cost assumption. Read Why? before relying on the number.
Fail
At least one reading does not meet this screen. You can still inspect the pool and save why you skipped it.
How do liquidity shapes work?

Choose the range first. The shape decides where your liquidity sits inside it.

Spot

Spreads liquidity evenly across the selected bins. A straightforward starting point for comparing ranges.

Curve

Concentrates more liquidity near the centre. More depends on price staying near those bins.

Bid-Ask

Weights the edges more heavily. One-sided bids below price buy as it falls; asks above price sell as it rises.

These diagrams show relative weights, not returns. Meteora strategy reference ↗

Price has to reach your bins
Resting bids below price earn no swap fees until swaps reach them. A wider range spreads your budget further; it cannot prevent losses.
Pool fees versus your fees
DLMM fees depend on your share of the bins used by swaps. Total TVL includes liquidity away from price. The pool’s fee APR is a past-data comparison, not your expected return.
Fees, prices and token checks
Price / LP P&L + fees
The terminal separates changes in position value from claimed and unclaimed fees. Total P&L combines them; network costs and incomplete history are labelled separately.
Reading volume
Compare recent windows and check whether the pool is old enough to fill those windows. Buying and selling both create volume; rising volume does not establish future price direction.
Check the token identity
A ticker, stock-themed name or Token-2022 program does not establish backing, leverage or a redemption right. Inspect the mint, issuer and transfer fees in the pool details.
What is a devnet stamp?

An optional public receipt of a saved paper event. Phantom signs a Memo on Solana devnet, using test SOL for the network fee. It does not create a real position or change your paper funds. Paper features work without a wallet.
